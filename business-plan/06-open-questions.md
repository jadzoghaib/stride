# 06 — Open Questions

The decisions I cannot make for you, each with the reason it matters, the
options, and my recommendation. **These are the agenda for our discussion.**

---

## A. Monetisation

### A1 — Take rate on fan revenue: 15% or 20%?

| | 15% | 20% |
|---|---|---|
| Athlete keeps | 85% | 80% |
| Y7 revenue | €13.91M | €18.54M |
| Positioning | "We take less than OnlyFans" | Parity, no story |

**Worth €4.6M at Y7.** The largest single lever in the model.

**My recommendation: 15%,** and say so loudly. Athlete acquisition is your only
real CAC, and a take rate an athlete repeats to their teammates is cheaper than
buying them. Revisit at Series A when the network effect, not the price, is the
reason to stay.

### A2 — Is Stride free for athletes forever?

You said free for athletes. That is right for acquisition, but it forecloses a
high-margin stream (athlete Pro tools: deeper analytics, competitor benchmarking,
media kit export).

**My recommendation: free forever for the core**, with paid tools *only* if
athletes ask for them. Charging the supply side of a marketplace before it is
dense is how marketplaces die.

### A3 — Minimum tier price?

The €4.99 tier retains 47% of our take after payment costs; €9.99 retains 64%.

**My recommendation: €9.99 default, €4.99 available but not suggested,** and
push the €89 season pass hard. This is worth more than a take-rate change and
costs nothing.

## B. Product scope

### B1 — Do we host video, or link out?

The decision that turns a data product into a content platform.

| | Host it | Link out |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Full PPV + subscription | Subscription only, weaker |
| Cost | Storage, transcode, egress, moderation | Near zero |
| Risk | Moderation liability, DSA obligations | Minimal |
| Differentiation | Real — this is the product | Thin |

**My recommendation: text and photo in P1, video only after the P1 data
justifies it.** See the sequencing in [05](05-product-gaps.md#sequenced-build).
Do not build a transcoding pipeline to test whether anyone will pay.

### B2 — Adults-only at launch?

**My recommendation: yes, and it is not close.** Under-18 athletes keep profiles
and analytics; monetisation unlocks at 18. This is the difference between a
regulatory conversation and an existential one.

### B3 — Sponsorship payments through Stride, or record-only?

Today the product records a deal but no money moves. Sponsors may prefer to pay
by invoice, outside the platform.

| | Payments through Stride | Record-only |
|---|---|---|
| Take rate | Enforceable | Trust-based, leaks |
| Sponsor friction | Higher (procurement, PO processes) | Lower |
| Escrow, dispute protection for athletes | Possible | No |

**My recommendation: through Stride, with invoicing for deals above ~€5k.**
Leakage on a record-only model is the standard way marketplace take rates go to
zero.

## C. Market and partnerships

### C1 — Spain only, or Iberia from the start?

**My recommendation: Spain only through the pre-seed gate.** One market, one
language, one tax regime, one federation network. Portugal in Y2 as the cheapest
possible test of whether the model travels.

### C2 — Which sport first?

Concentration beats breadth for a cold start: fans of one sport talk to each
other, and clubs in one federation talk to each other.

Candidates worth discussing: **football (lower divisions)** — huge but crowded;
**basketball (ACB/LEB)** — strong club structure, national following;
**endurance/triathlon/trail** — highly engaged, content-hungry, underserved,
athletes already self-publish; **padel** — fast-growing, Spanish-centred,
commercially hungry.

**My instinct is endurance or padel**, not football. Football's long tail is
served by agents; endurance athletes already write training blogs for free and
have nowhere to charge for them. But you know this market and I do not — this is
a genuine question.

### C3 — Anchor athlete: equity or cash?

**My recommendation: 1% advisory equity, 2-year vest, 6-month cliff, +0.5%
performance trigger.** Costs no cash at the point when cash is scarcest, and
aligns them to the exit rather than to the launch.

### C4 — Compete with agencies or partner with them?

Agencies serve the top 1% and ignore your segment. They are not competitors —
they are a **distribution channel** (Scout Agency at €999/mo) and eventually an
acquisition path.

**My recommendation: partner, and price a plan for them.**

## D. Financial

### D1 — Raise the €777k the plan needs, or the €2.4M the rounds imply?

**My recommendation: take the non-dilutive stack first** (ENISA + Neotec,
€300–500k), then a smaller pre-seed. Every euro of grant money is a euro of
equity retained, and the plan's capital need is genuinely modest.

### D2 — How much founder salary in Y1?

Zero maximises runway; zero also burns the founder. The model assumes unpaid Y1
and €45k from Y2, which is €50k of forgone earnings in Y1 alone.

**Worth discussing honestly:** the plan's capital need is small enough that
€24k of founder salary in Y1 changes very little in the model and quite a lot in
whether you can sustain the work.

### D3 — Which valuation do we present?

**My recommendation: lead with the exit-multiple method discounted back
(€20–32M), and present the DCF (€7.9M) as the conservative floor.** Showing
both, and explaining why they differ, is more credible than showing whichever is
larger. An examiner who spots that a perpetuity-growth DCF was applied to a
57%-growth company will discount everything else you said.

## E. The question underneath all of them

**Is Stride a creator platform with a sponsorship feature, or a sponsorship
marketplace with a creator feature?**

The product as built is unambiguously the second. The plan as modelled is
unambiguously the first — 60% of Y7 revenue is fan money.

Both are viable businesses. They need different teams, different investors,
different first hires and different anchor athletes. What they cannot be is
undecided, because every P0–P4 sequencing choice depends on the answer.

**My reading:** the analytics engine is the defensible asset and nobody else has
it, but analytics alone is a feature, not a company. The creator model is where
the revenue and the growth are. **So: a creator platform that wins because its
analytics make the second payer possible** — which is precisely the thing
OnlyFans cannot do.

That is the sentence the whole plan should be built to prove. Tell me if you
read it differently, because if you do, several documents here need to change.
